The Certified Junior Cybersecurity Associate (CJCA) is Hack The Box's entry-level certification. I sat the exam, submitted my report, and passed. This is my honest take on whether it's worth your time and money.

**Disclosure:** I received the Silver Annual subscription (which includes the exam vouchers) as an HTB Ambassador. Everything below is my own opinion.

I won't go through the exam rules or logistics. HTB has documented those well on their own site. This is my experience and the practical information I'd want before starting.


## What's Included

The CJCA exam voucher comes bundled with the **Silver Annual** subscription, which is **$550**. (Updated Price)

CJCA is a late addition to that subscription. Silver Annual was built mainly around the CPTS, CDSA, CWES and COAE (latest addition) certifications, and HTB bundled CJCA in once it was released. That means your $550 gets you:

- A full year of access to the content for all of those certifications
- One voucher for CJCA
- A second voucher for **one** of CPTS, CDSA, CWES or COAE

To be eligible for the exam you have to complete the **Junior Cybersecurity Analyst** job role path on HTB Academy.

I completed that path a long time ago, as part of my own plan to work through every Academy module. Because of that, I can't give you a realistic estimate of how long the path takes from scratch, and I'd rather not guess.

What I can say is that the module content is more than enough to pass the exam. You don't need outside material.

## The Exam Format

The exam runs for **five days**, report writing included, and has two parts:

- **Lab part:** offensive
- **SOC part:** defensive

I'll leave the details there, since HTB covers the format themselves.

## Exam Experience

I'll start with the one rough patch.

When I started, my VPN kept disconnecting at random. I went through several resets and switched VPN configurations more than once before it settled. I also opened a support ticket, and it took about six hours to get a response. That was probably a timezone gap, so I won't hold it against them too hard. Once I was sorted out, support added one extra day to my exam timer to make up for the disruption. I didn't end up needing it, but it was a fair way to handle it.

From that point on, the environment was stable and the exam flowed without issues.

On difficulty, the exam felt like a breeze to me, and that includes the blue team part. For context, I finished everything in roughly **five hours total**. That is my experience and not a benchmark. I had done the path before and I do this kind of work regularly. The five days are more than enough time for someone newer.


## The Report

The offensive part requires a report, and this was one of the better parts of the experience. HTB provides **SysReptor templates** you can use, so you aren't left guessing at structure or formatting, and you can spend your time on the findings.

## Where It Could Improve

Two things I'd change:

**1. A report for the blue team part.** The defensive half of the exam would be stronger if it required you to prepare an actual report. Documenting findings and communicating them is a core part of defensive work, and it's the skill I'd most like the exam to test on that side.

**2. More content tying attacks to their detections.** The modules cover a lot, but I'd like more material that connects offensive techniques to how they show up in logs and alerts. Seeing both sides of the same attack is what makes blue team work click for a lot of people, and it would also make the path more useful for those who start on the offensive side.

## Value for Money

Judged on CJCA alone, $550 would be a lot to pay for an entry-level exam. But that isn't what you're buying. The subscription's real value is a year of access to the content behind the larger certifications, plus a voucher for one of them on top of the CJCA voucher. CJCA being bundled in makes it an easy first step into that.

On that basis, the value for money is great.


## Who Should Take It

**If you're getting into cybersecurity and want to test the waters:** I would recommend both the path and the exam, 100%. The modules cover a lot of ground, and the exam tests it comprehensively. It's a very good way to open your eyes to what the field involves, offensive and defensive.

**If you already have experience:** expect it to be easy, as it was for me. But the voucher comes with the subscription either way, so it costs you nothing extra, and it's a good way to test your fundamentals. It won't stretch you, but it will show you whether the basics you've been relying on are as solid as you think.

**If you're comparing it with other entry-level certs:** a cert like eJPT is mostly offensive. CJCA gives you both an offensive and a defensive assessment, so the two aren't a like-for-like comparison.


## What to Do Next

Once you've finished CJCA, you can pick your next direction and start the matching path: **Pentesting**, **Web Hacking**, **Blue Team**, or even **AI**. The second voucher in your subscription covers the exam for whichever you choose, so there's a clear next step already paid for.


## Bottom Line

CJCA does what an entry-level certification should. The path teaches a wide base, and the exam checks that you absorbed it across both sides of security. It isn't hard, and it isn't meant to be. Bundled into Silver Annual, it also sits at the start of a full year of content and a second voucher.

I'd like to see a report requirement on the defensive side and more detection-focused content in the modules. Short of that, I have no hesitation recommending it to anyone starting out.

I passed, and I'm glad I sat it. Whether you're just starting out or already have some experience, it's worth doing.
